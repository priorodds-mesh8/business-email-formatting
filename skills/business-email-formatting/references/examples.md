# Format choices and examples

All names, terms, and events below are fictional. Reuse the structure only when it fits the actual message.

## A dense request becomes a paragraph and a list

Input:

> Hi Alex, please approve the revised scope by October 30 so we can start on November 2. The revision includes weekly account research, a shared review dashboard, and one team review each Friday. The fee remains $12,000. Data purchases are separate. Please reply with your approval or the changes you need.

Formatted draft:

Subject: Approval needed by October 30: revised scope

Hi Alex,

Please approve the revised scope by October 30 so we can start on November 2.

The revision includes:

- Weekly account research.
- A shared review dashboard.
- One team review each Friday.

The fee remains $12,000. Data purchases are separate.

Please reply with your approval or the changes you need.

The list groups deliverables. The fee and exclusion remain together. No takeaways section is needed.

## A short reply stays short

Input: The client asks whether Tuesday at 10 AM Central works. Sam confirms that it does.

Draft:

Hi Alex,

Tuesday at 10 AM Central works for me.

Thanks,
Sam

Do not add headings, bullets, a new request, or a claim that a calendar invitation is sent.

## An update separates status and action

Input: The dashboard is ready for review. The export is incomplete because source data is missing. Alex must confirm the date range before work can continue. No deadline is supplied.

Draft:

Hi Alex,

The dashboard is ready for review. The export remains incomplete because source data is missing.

Please confirm the date range so I can continue the export work.

This update needs two short paragraphs. Do not force three bullets or invent a deadline.

## A recap gives actions their own section

Input: The team chooses a two-week pilot. Morgan owns the account list, due November 3. Sam owns the sample report, due November 5. The data purchase decision remains open.

Draft:

Hi team,

We agreed to a two-week pilot. The data purchase decision remains open.

**Next steps**

- Morgan: Send the account list by November 3.
- Sam: Send the sample report by November 5.

The owners and dates come from the input. The draft does not invent an owner for the open decision.

## Takeaways help only when detail follows

For a long update with several topics, start with its main outcome. Add a small takeaway list if the reader needs an overview. Then group the necessary details under descriptive labels. Do not repeat every detail in the takeaway list.

For an apology or a sensitive client message, preserve empathy and explanation. Do not turn the entire message into bullets solely to shorten it.
